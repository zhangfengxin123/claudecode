Executive Summary

This comprehensive analysis examines dollar spot disease Clarireedia spp. severity in creeping bentgrass across 48 research locations from 2021 to 2025. By integrating disease assessment data 2,508 records with meteorological observations 7,933 records, this study identifies critical patterns in disease dynamics.

Key Findings:

  • Strong Seasonal Pattern: Disease severity peaks dramatically in August average severity: 157 compared to May 6.6, representing a 24x increase during peak season
  • High Geographic Variability: Wisconsin shows the highest average severity 224.4, followed by Maryland 259.8 at specific locations, while Pennsylvania demonstrates the most consistent control 26.8
  • Limited Weather Correlation: Temperature +0.025, humidity +0.082, and precipitation -0.028 show minimal direct correlation with disease severity, suggesting complex multi-factor influences
  • Highly Variable Severity: 69.8% of cases show minimal to moderate severity <50, but extreme outliers reach up to 7,210 severity units
  • Temporal Trends: 2025 shows the highest average severity 116.3, potentially indicating changing disease pressure or monitoring conditions

4.3 Interpretation of Weak Correlations

The minimal direct correlations between weather parameters and disease severity suggest:

Complex Disease Dynamics:

1. Time-Lag Effects: Disease may respond to conditions days or weeks before assessment

2. Cumulative Stress: Disease development may depend on cumulative environmental stress rather than instantaneous conditions

3. Microclimate Variations: Local canopy conditions may differ from weather station measurements

4. Pathogen Interactions: Multiple pathogen species or strains with different environmental optima

5. Plant Health Factors: Plant nutrition, mowing practices, and cultural practices override weather effects

Implications for Management:

  • Preventive Approach: Disease management cannot rely solely on weather-based predictions
  • Integrated Pest Management: Combine monitoring with cultural practices and preventive treatments
  • Site-Specific Strategies: Local conditions and management history may be more predictive than general weather patterns

Based on this analysis, the following factors predict high dollar spot severity:

High-Risk Conditions:

  • Temporal: August assessments, 2-3 months after planting
  • Geographic: Wisconsin, Maryland locations, specific high-pressure sites
  • Environmental: Warm temperatures 22-25°C combined with high humidity 70%
  • Cultural: Creeping bentgrass, frequent mowing, high nitrogen fertility

Low-Risk Conditions:

  • Temporal: Early season May or late season November assessments

  • Environmental: Cooler temperatures <18°C, lower humidity
  • Management: Preventive fungicide programs, resistant cultivars
