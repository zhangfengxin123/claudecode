Global Energy Outlook 2024: Record Consumption Amid Climate Crisis

Executive Summary

2023 marked another year of paradox in global energy markets: record consumption of fossil fuels and record greenhouse gas emissions, but also record generation from renewables. The Statistical Review of World Energy 2024 reveals a world still heavily dependent on traditional energy sources, with fossil fuels accounting for 81.5%of global energy consumption, while renewables reached a new high of 14.6%share.

The energy transition progresses slowly, with China adding more renewable capacity than the rest of the world combined, yet global CO₂ emissions breached 35 GtCO₂efor the first time in history.

Conclusion

The Statistical Review of World Energy 2024 presents a complex picture: a world simultaneously achieving record highs in energy consumption, fossil fuel use, greenhouse gas emissions, and renewable generation. The energy transition is progressing, but not at the pace required to meet climate goals.

The Global Southnow accounts for 56%of total energy consumption, growing at twice the global average rate, while maintaining per capita consumption far below developed regions. This presents both challenges and opportunities: the potential for rapid emissions growth if development follows fossil fuel pathways, or the possibility of leapfrogging directly to clean energy systems.

As we navigate this critical decade, the choices made by major economies, the pace of technological innovation, and the success of policy coordination will determine whether we achieve a sustainable energy future or continue on our current trajectory toward climate disruption.
