Executive Summary

This comprehensive quantitative analysis examines 120,000 payment transactions across 5,896 merchants, providing institutional-grade insights into fraud patterns, transaction characteristics, and merchant risk profiles. The overall fraud rate of 0.332% is approximately one-third of the 1% high-risk threshold established by Visa and Mastercard, indicating effective fraud prevention measures.

Key findings:

  • Distribution Characteristics: Transaction amounts exhibit significant positive skewness 3.03 and high kurtosis 17.76, confirming a heavy-tailed distribution with significant outliers
  • Fraud Detection: Merchant fraud rate is the strongest and only statistically significant predictor of transaction fraud r=0.2246, p0.001
  • Transaction Analysis: Fraud amounts are not statistically different from non-fraud amounts t-test p=0.5652, suggesting that transaction value alone is not a fraud indicator
  • Industry Variation: Transaction amounts vary significantly across industries ANOVA p0.001, with jewelry showing the highest average 736.92 and digital goods the lowest 89.88
  • Model Performance: Linear regression explains 5.05% of variance in fraud, with merchant fraud rate driving almost all explanatory power

Industry Context and Benchmarks

Industry Chargeback and Fraud Benchmarks

Industry research reveals substantial variation in chargeback and fraud rates across sectors:

  • Education: 4.79% average chargeback rate
  • Travel: 4.68% average chargeback rate
  • Digital Goods: 3.62% average chargeback rate
  • Gaming: 3.41% average chargeback rate
  • Health/Beauty: 2.73% average chargeback rate

Global benchmarks indicate that merchants exceeding a 1% chargeback rate are classified as high-risk by Visa and Mastercard. The average merchant wins only 45% of chargeback disputes, with friendly fraud accounting for approximately 70% of all chargebacks.

Comparative Analysis

Our dataset's fraud rates are significantly below industry benchmarks across all sectors:

  • Electronics: Observed 0.33% vs. industry Travel: Observed 0.28% vs. industry 4.68%
  • Digital Goods: Observed 0.26% vs. industry 3.62%
  • Health/Beauty: Observed 0.28% vs. industry 2.73%

This substantial performance gap suggests effective fraud prevention measures currently in place.

Feature Engineering

Engineered Features and Business Rationale

The following features were engineered to capture business insights relevant to fraud detection:

Ratio Features

  • logamount: Log-transformed transaction amount

Growth/Change Features

  • merchantfraudrate: Calculated as fraudcount / totaltxns per merchant

Temporal Features

  • isweekend: Binary indicator for weekend transactions Saturday/Sunday
  • isnighthour: Binary indicator for transactions between 12am-6am

Volatility/Risk Features

  • amountdeviationfrommean: amount - merchantmean / merchantmean
  • amountzscore: amount - merchantmean / merchantstd

Scale Features

  • merchanttxncount: Total transactions per merchant

Statistical Tests

Normality Testing

Transaction Amount Distribution

Null Hypothesis: Transaction amounts follow a normal distribution

  • Shapiro-Wilk Testn=5000: W=0.6831, p0.001
  • Jarque-Bera Test: JB=1,760,811.04, p0.001

The high skewness 3.03 and extreme kurtosis 17.76 confirm the presence of a heavy-tailed distribution with significant outliers typical of financial transaction data.

Log-Transformed Amount Distribution

  • Shapiro-Wilk Testn=5000: W=0.9986, p=0.0003

Log-transformation reduces skewness to -0.02 and kurtosis to -0.28, bringing the distribution much closer to normal, though still statistically non-normal.

Hypothesis Testing

T-Test: Fraud vs Non-Fraud Amounts

Hypotheses:

  • H0: μfraud = μnon-fraud
  • Ha: μfraud ≠ μnon-fraud

Results:

  • T-statistic: 0.5756
  • P-value: 0.5652
  • Decision: Cannot reject null hypothesis
  • Interpretation: Fraud amounts are not significantly different from non-fraud amounts

This result confirms that transaction amount alone is not a reliable indicator of fraud.

ANOVA: Transaction Amounts by Industry

Hypotheses:

  • H0: μelectronics = μfashion = ... = μdigitalgoods
  • Ha: At least one industry mean differs

Results:

  • F-statistic: 7,782.20
  • P-value: p0.001
  • Decision: Reject null hypothesis
  • Interpretation: At least one industry has significantly different transaction amounts

This confirms substantial variation in transaction values across industries, consistent with the observed range from 89.88 digital goods to 736.92 jewelry.

Chi-Square Test: Fraud Independence by Payment Method

Hypotheses:

  • H0: Fraud is independent of payment method
  • Ha: Fraud is associated with payment method

Results:

  • Chi-square: 1.5084
  • P-value: 0.4704
  • Degrees of freedom: 2
  • Decision: Cannot reject independence
  • Interpretation: Payment method is not significantly associated with fraud

The similar fraud rates across card 0.343%, ACH 0.305%, and wallet 0.279% indicate that fraud attempts are distributed evenly across payment methods.

The absence of a clear time-based fraud pattern suggests that fraud attempts are distributed throughout the day rather than concentrated in specific time windows.

This variation justifies the implementation of merchant-specific risk monitoring and tiered fraud prevention strategies.

Conclusions

Key Findings Summary

1. Fraud Rate Performance: The overall fraud rate of 0.332% is approximately one-third of the 1% high-risk threshold and significantly below industry benchmarks 2.73-4.79% for high-risk industries, indicating effective fraud prevention measures.

2. Distribution Characteristics: Transaction amounts are heavily right-skewed skewness=3.03 with fat tails kurtosis=17.76, necessitating log-transformation for proper statistical analysis.

3. Predictor Significance: Merchant-level fraud history is the strongest and only statistically significant predictor of transaction fraud r=0.2246, p0.001. Transaction-level features amount, time, weekend show no predictive power.

4. Industry Variations: Transaction amounts vary significantly across industries ANOVA p0.001, but fraud rates are relatively consistent across payment methods.

5. Model Performance: The linear regression model explains 5.05% of variance in fraud, with merchant fraud rate driving almost all explanatory power.

Business Implications

Risk-Based Merchant Management

  • Implement tiered monitoring based on historical merchant fraud rates
  • High-risk merchants above 0.5% fraud rate should receive enhanced scrutiny
  • Consider progressive intervention strategies for merchants approaching the 1% threshold

Transaction-Level Screening

  • Current transaction-level features amount, time provide minimal predictive value
  • Consider additional features: customer behavior patterns, device fingerprinting, velocity checks
  • Implement anomaly detection based on merchant-specific patterns

Industry-Specific Approaches

  • Industries with higher average transaction values jewelry, electronics may benefit from additional verification for high-value transactions
  • Consider industry-specific risk scoring models that account for sector-specific fraud patterns

Machine Learning Opportunity

  • Linear model's low R-squared 0.0505 suggests potential for improvement using non-linear models
  • Consider ensemble methods random forests, gradient boosting or neural networks
  • Feature engineering focusing on customer behavior and transaction patterns could improve performance

Limitations and Future Work

Limitations

  • Limited to transaction and merchant-level features; customer behavior, geographic location, and device information could enhance predictive power
  • Linear models may not capture non-linear relationships and feature interactions
  • Analysis does not account for fraud trends over time
  • Results should be validated on out-of-sample data

Future Research Directions

  • Time-series analysis to identify emerging fraud patterns
  • Customer behavior analysis to detect account takeover and synthetic identity fraud
  • Network analysis to identify fraud rings and connected merchants
  • A/B testing of intervention strategies for high-risk merchants
  • Investigation of merchant onboarding characteristics that predict future fraud risk
