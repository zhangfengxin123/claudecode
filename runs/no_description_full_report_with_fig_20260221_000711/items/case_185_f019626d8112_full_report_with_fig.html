China Banks and Property 2026 Outlook: Is the Worst Behind?

Executive Summary

UBS Global Research's 2026 Greater China Conference reveals a cautiously optimisticoutlook for China's economy, with banks and property sectors showing signs of stabilization despite ongoing challenges. The report suggests that while the worst of the downturn may be behind us, significant headwinds remain, particularly in the property sector.

Key Headlines:

  • GDP growth target set at 4.5-5.0% with UBS forecasting 4.5%
  • 10bps rate cut expected in 2026, limited by bank NIM constraints
  • Property prices forecast to decline 10% in 2026, 5% in 2027
  • Banking sector shows improved revenue outlook with narrow NIM decline
  • High-dividend banks CITIC-H, CCB-H, BOC-H, ICBC-H preferred for 2026

Growth Targets and Forecasts

China's economic landscape in 2026 is characterized by a moderately positive outlookwith several key indicators showing improvement:

GDP Growth Projections:

  • Target Range: 4.5-5.0% consensus, with policy aiming for "close to 5.0%"
  • UBS Forecast: 4.5% real GDP growth
  • Long-term Requirement: 4.2% average growth needed for 2026-2035 15th Five-Year Plan

The divergence in expert views regarding achievability of these targets reflects the complex balance of:

  • Optimistic view: Strong exports momentum 5% growth forecast in USD terms, resilient domestic consumption
  • Cautious view: Need to return to infrastructure investments 2025: -2.2% to offset weakening consumption and net exports

Inflation Dynamics

A notable bright spot is the easing deflationary pressure:

  • CPI: Expected to rise to 0.5% in 2026
  • PPI: Forecast to narrow decline to -1% to 0% range, potentially reaching zero by year-end
  • GDP Deflator: Less negative trend expected to support corporate earnings and fiscal revenue

However, sustainability remains uncertain as the anti-involution campaignrepresents administrative measures addressing short-term symptoms rather than the long-term structural supply-demand gap.

NIM Pressure

Banks face continued NIM compression, though the rate of decline is expected to narrow:

  • 9M25 NIM decline: -11 bps YoY
  • 2026 forecast: Single-digit decline

The industry self-discipline mechanismhas helped contain pricing competition by setting floor rates on loans, though government-related lending faces potential downside pressure.

Bearish Outlook for 2026

The property sector faces a challenging year aheadwith continued downward pressure:

2026 Forecasts:

  • Property price decline: -10%
  • Property sales decline: -5% to -6% GFA/value terms
  • Property investment decline: -10% to -5% narrowing from -17.2% in 2025
  • Secondary housing prices: Unlikely to bottom in 2026

2027 Outlook:

  • Property price decline: -5% further moderation

Bearish Case Rationale

Three key factors support the bearish outlook:

1. Rental Yield Gap: Rental yields have not improved due to rental declines

2. Supply Overhang: Social housing completion expected to increase in 2026-2027

3. Income Expectations: Consumer income expectations have not recovered yet

Worst-Case Scenario:

If rental yields match mortgage rates, property prices could have an additional 30-40% downsidefrom current levels.

Policy Constraints

Policy tools available to stabilize property prices appear limited:

Low Probability Policies not expected in 2026:

  • Large-scale inventory purchase by central government
  • Fiscal subsidy on existing mortgage interest rates

Reasons for Policy Hesitation:

  • Mortgage rate subsidy: Under discussion but far from execution
  • Qiushi Article: Viewed as propaganda rather than new policy stance

Distressed Developer Insights

A distressed property developer with 720k units completed and Rmb 250bn onshore debt reveals:

  • NPL Recognition Delay: 50%+ of outstanding bank loans still classified as normal
  • Regulatory Forbearance: Banks have strong incentives to allow roll-over of project loans
  • Collateral Coverage: Majority of developer loans covered by collateral value
  • AMC Requirements: Asset Management Companies require 8% IRR and assume 30% property price decline
  • Exit Timeline: Process for banks to exit via discounted sale to AMC could be very long

Selective Opportunities

Despite the bearish outlook, selective opportunities exist:

1. Luxury Housing: Remains demand with certain entry barriers

2. Well-Managed Malls: CR Mixc continues to outperform market

UBS Stock Pick: CR Mixc preferred in property space

Monetary Policy: Limited Flexibility

Expected Action:10bps rate cut in 2026

Constraints:

  • Banks' stretched NIM levels remain key bottleneck
  • Real interest rate still high despite easing deflation pressure
  • Neutral interest rate may have fallen to zero

Rationale for Cautious Approach:

  • 30-year central government bond yield risk skewed heavily to upside
  • Improved inflation outlook reduces urgency for aggressive easing
  • Quasi-fiscal policies expected to be broadened

Fiscal Policy: Focus on Efficiency

Key Principles December CEWC:

  • "Promoting quality and efficiency" vs "extraordinary counter-cyclical measures"
  • Maintain "necessary levels" of fiscal deficit, government debt, and total spending
  • Focus on efficiency rather than scale expansion

Trade-in Policy Impact:

  • Rmb 300bn subsidies with limited multiplier effect
  • 2025 social retail sales: +3.7% vs +3.5% in 2024
  • Expert recommendation: Gradual phase-out

LGFV Debt Resolution

Sichuan Province Success Story:

  • Chengdu: Issues 60% of provincial LGFV bonds
  • Debt Structure: 70% bank loans, 20% bonds, 10% non-standard credit
  • Debt Growth: 10-15% 2022-23 → 5% 1H25 → further decline expected in 2026
  • Public Defaults: Zero due to effective provincial coordination

Is the Worst Behind? The Verdict

Conclusion: Partially, but challenges remain

What's Improving:

1. Macroeconomic: Smaller drag from property sector, strong exports momentum

2. Inflation: Easing deflationary pressure, PPI potentially reaching zero

3. Banking: NIM decline narrowing, revenue outlook improving

4. LGFV: Debt growth moderating, effective coordination preventing defaults

5. Property: Price decline narrowing, investment drop reducing

What's Still Concerning:

1. Property Prices: Additional 30-40% downside potential in worst case

2. Policy Constraints: Limited fiscal expansion, NIM constraints on rate cuts

3. Asset Quality: Developer NPL cleanup will take time 50%+ still classified as normal

4. Consumption: Not a key growth driver in 2026

5. Geopolitics: Trade tensions with EU and US uncertainty

Final Thoughts

The UBS research presents a nuanced pictureof China's banking and property sectors in 2026. While the worst of the rapid deterioration appears to have passed, the road to full recovery will be gradual and uneven.

Key Message for Investors:

  • Patience is essential: The adjustment process will take time
  • Dividends matter: High-yield banks provide attractive risk-adjusted returns
  • Selectivity is critical: Focus on quality names with strong fundamentals
  • Policy watching: Monitor policy effectiveness and implementation closely

The data suggests that 2026 will be a transition year—not a dramatic turnaround, but a steady march toward stabilization. Investors who maintain a disciplined, selective approachfocused on high-quality banks with strong dividends and manageable risk profiles will be well-positioned for the eventual recovery.
