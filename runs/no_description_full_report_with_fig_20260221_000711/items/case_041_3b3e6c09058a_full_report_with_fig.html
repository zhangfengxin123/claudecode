AEMO Electricity Market Bid Analysis - December 2025

Executive Summary

This report provides a comprehensive analysis of the Australian Energy Market Operator AEMO bid data for December 3, 2025. The dataset contains 278,593 bid records representing market participant offers across various bid types, price bands, and directional flows. After filtering for valid data, 2,048 records were analyzed to uncover key market dynamics, participant behavior, and pricing patterns.

Key Findings:

  • HYDROTAS leads market participation with 176 bids 8.6% of filtered data
  • Energy bids dominate at 25.9%, followed closely by ancillary service bids
  • Generation bids comprise 63% of all bids, reflecting supply-side market activity
  • Price bands show exponential increase from band 1 -$1,064.90 to band 10 21,617.44
  • Daily and rebid bids are nearly evenly split 49.6% vs 50.4%, indicating active market adjustments

Pricing Dynamics

Negative Price Strategy:Lower price bands 1-3 show significant negative values, with PRICEBAND1 averaging -244.70 and reaching as low as -1,064.90. This reflects:

  • Must-Run Generation:Some generators e.g., wind, solar offer to pay for generation to maintain minimum operating levels
  • Flexibility Services:Participants offer to reduce output negative prices to help manage oversupply conditions
  • Market Entry Strategies:Strategic bidding to ensure dispatch in competitive markets

Exponential Price Growth:Mean prices increase exponentially across bands, from -244.70 band 1 to 17,071.03 band 10. This reflects:

  • Scarcity Pricing:Higher bands represent willingness to pay premium prices during system stress
  • Capacity Constraints:Limited generation capacity commands higher prices during peak demand
  • Risk Premium:Participants price in the risk of not being dispatched in lower bands

Volatility Increase:Price ranges expand significantly across bands:

  • Band 1: 1,076 range
  • Band 5: 5,351 range
  • Band 10: 21,612 range

This widening range indicates increasing price volatility at higher scarcity levels, typical of electricity markets during peak demand.

Cap Price Behavior:PRICEBAND10 median of 20,300 approaches the market cap, indicating many participants offer at the maximum price in the final band, representing their willingness to supply under any condition at the capped price.

Market Implications

Generator Economics:The negative pricing in lower bands suggests:

  • Renewable generators often bid negative to ensure dispatch
  • Thermal generators bid across full range to recover fixed costs
  • Must-run assets use negative bids to maintain system stability

Price Volatility:The exponential increase creates opportunities for:

  • Energy arbitrage buy low, sell high
  • Storage operation charge at negative prices, discharge at peak
  • Demand response activation during high-price periods
