101 Formulaic Alphas: Empirical Analysis of Quantitative Trading Strategies

Executive Summary

This report presents a comprehensive analysis of 101 real-life quantitative trading alphas from the WorldQuant dataset 2010-2013. Key findings reveal a strong power-law relationship between returns and volatilityR

The analysis demonstrates that volatility managementis critical for alpha performance, while turnover optimizationhas minimal impact on returns. The low correlation structure enables effective alpha combination, making it possible to construct robust multi-alpha portfolios with superior risk-adjusted returns.

1. Prioritize Volatility Management:

  • Use volatility targeting to achieve desired return levels
  • Apply volatility normalization when combining alphas
  • Implement volatility scaling for risk-adjusted optimization

2. Ignore Turnover Optimization:

  • Do not penalize high-turnout alphasin portfolio construction
  • Focus on risk-adjusted returns, not turnover metrics
  • Trading costs should be addressed separately, not through turnover constraints

3. Leverage Low Correlations:

  • Combine many alphaswithout diversification concerns
  • Use correlation structure to optimize portfolio weights
  • Implement "mega-alpha" approach with hundreds of combined signals

Alpha Research Implications

1. Strategy Development:

  • Focus on signal quality and predictive power, not execution speed
  • High-volatility strategies can be more attractive after scaling
  • Low-turnout strategies are not inherently superior

2. Backtesting Methodology:

  • Always analyze volatility-return relationships
  • Test for turnover dependence in returns
  • Report correlation structure alongside individual alpha performance

3. Risk Modeling:

  • Build factor models focused on volatility risk factors
  • Turnover-based liquidity factors add little value
  • Correlation structure is stable and predictable

Execution and Trading

1. Trading Cost Management:

  • Address transaction costs directly, not through turnover reduction
  • Optimize execution quality independently of turnover
  • Consider portfolio-level crossing for cost reduction

2. Infrastructure Implications:

  • Support for high-turnout strategies is not critical
  • Volatility scaling infrastructure is more important
  • Correlation analysis tools should be prioritized
