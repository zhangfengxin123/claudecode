Executive Summary

This report presents a comprehensive quantitative analysis of the relationship between egg prices, Consumer Price Index CPI, and Economic Policy Uncertainty EPU index. The analysis covers data from February 2021 to January 2026 60 monthly observations, examining price dynamics, correlation patterns, and regression models to understand factors influencing egg market behavior.

Key Findings

  • Egg Price Statistics:Mean price of 2.95 with significant volatility standard deviation: 1.09, coefficient of variation: 36.96%
  • Strong CPI Correlation:Egg prices show strong positive correlation with CPI r = 0.648, p 0.001, indicating general inflation drives egg prices
  • Moderate EPU Relationship:Moderate positive correlation between egg prices and EPU r = 0.601, p 0.001, suggesting policy uncertainty impacts egg market
  • Model Performance:Regression Model 1 Egg Price Distribution Characteristics:Egg prices exhibit non-normal distribution Shapiro-Wilk: W = 0.922, p = 0.001, right-skewed with occasional spikes
  • Inflation Comparison:No statistically significant difference between egg price changes 1.74%/month and CPI inflation 0.37%/month in paired t-test p = 0.333

Economic Policy Uncertainty

The EPU index, developed by Baker et al. 2016, measures economic and policy-related uncertainties. Research indicates:

  • EPU has been approximately two times its long-run average since 2008
  • Policy uncertainty innovations foreshadow declines in investment, output, and employment
  • EPU serves as a leading indicator for market volatility

Model Comparison

  • Model 1 levels performs much better than Model 2 changes
  • Price levels are more predictable than price changes
  • Monthly price changes dominated by idiosyncratic factors weather, disease, regional shocks
  • Only lagged EPU shows significant effect on price changes, suggesting delayed negative impact of policy uncertainty

Hypothesis Testing

Test 1: Egg Price Change vs. CPI Inflation

Hypotheses:

  • H0: Meanegg change = MeanCPI inflation
  • H1: Meanegg change CPI inflation

Results:

  • Paired t-test: t = 0.977, p = 0.333
  • Mean egg price change: 1.74%/month
  • Mean CPI inflation: 0.37%/month

Conclusion:FAIL TO REJECT H0. Despite large point estimate difference, not statistically significant due to high volatility of egg price changes. Egg prices broadly follow general inflation in long run.

Test 2: EPU in High vs. Low Volatility Periods

Hypotheses:

  • H0: MeanEPU | high vol = MeanEPU | low vol
  • H1: MeanEPU | high vol EPU | low vol

Results:

  • Independent t-test: t = 1.812, p = 0.077
  • Mean EPU high vol: 218.71
  • Mean EPU low vol: 154.98

Conclusion:FAIL TO REJECT H0 marginally. While EPU is higher during high egg price volatility periods, difference not statistically significant at 5% level.

Conclusions and Implications

Summary of Findings

1. Strong Long-Run Relationships:Egg price levels show strong positive correlations with both CPI r = 0.648 and EPU r = 0.601, with regression model explaining 51.7% of variance

2. Weak Short-Term Predictability:Monthly egg price changes poorly explained by macroeconomic variables R-squared = 0.137, suggesting short-term dynamics driven by idiosyncratic factors

3. Non-Normal Distributions:Egg prices, CPI, and EPU exhibit non-normal distributions, consistent with trending time series with extreme events

4. Delayed EPU Effects:Lagged EPU shows significant negative relationship with egg price changes beta = -0.0007, p = 0.025, suggesting policy uncertainty shocks have delayed contractionary effects

5. No Significant Egg Inflation Premium:Despite point estimates suggesting higher egg price inflation, difference not statistically significant, indicating egg prices track general inflation in long run

Investment and Policy Implications

  • Risk Management:High volatility of egg price changes and weak short-term model suggest focus on hedging strategies rather than predicting monthly movements
  • Supply Chain Resilience:Strong correlation between EPU and egg prices indicates policy uncertainty as systematic risk factor; building resilient supply chains critical
  • Monitoring Early Indicators:Negative relationship between lagged EPU and egg price changes suggests elevated uncertainty may signal future price momentum reversals
  • Market Structure:Egg prices track CPI in long run but exhibit high short-term volatility, consistent with commodity markets determined by supply-demand fundamentals with temporary shocks

Limitations

  • Limited Sample Size:Only 60 monthly observations
  • Omitted Variables:Does not include supply-side factors feed prices, production costs, disease outbreaks, weather
  • Regional Aggregation:National average prices mask regional variation
  • Future Research:Include supply-side variables, disease indicators, regional differentials, longer historical period, structural break analysis
