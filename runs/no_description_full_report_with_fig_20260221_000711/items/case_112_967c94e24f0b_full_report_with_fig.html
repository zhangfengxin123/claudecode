AV Rollout Strategy

Uber is pursuing a hybrid network model, combining human drivers with AV fleets from partners like Waymo. The company aims to be the world's largest AV facilitator by 2029, with a target of 15 AV cities by end-2026.

Current AV Status:

  • AV penetration: 0.1% of global rideshare trips early stage
  • Target: 15 cities by end-2026 roughly even US/International split
  • Strategic goal: Largest AV facilitator globally by 2029

The deliberate rollout strategy prioritizes:

  • Geographic diversification not just US cities
  • Varied market types dense urban, suburban, international
  • Multiple AV partners reduces dependency risk
  • Gradual market share accumulation testing, learning, scaling

Superior AV Unit Economics

Uber reported compelling data from AV deployments in Austin and Atlanta, demonstrating superior economics compared to first-party deployments Waymo in San Francisco, Los Angeles, Phoenix:

Performance Advantages Austin/Atlanta:

  • 30% more trips per vehicle per day higher utilization
  • 25% faster ETAs better matching efficiency
  • First-time rider growth acceleration: +9 percentage points
  • Trips per rider growth acceleration: +3 percentage points

The 30% higher utilization and 25% faster ETAs suggest Uber's hybrid model creates efficiency advantages through:

  • Better marketplace liquidity combining AV and human supply
  • Dynamic deployment algorithms AVs positioned where demand is predictable
  • Reduced deadhead optimized routing across heterogeneous fleet

Demand Expansion Evidence:

The acceleration in first-time riders +9 pp and trips per rider +3 pp in AV zones suggests AVs expand the addressable market by:

  • Lowering prices autonomous vehicles reduce costs
  • Improving reliability machine consistency vs human variability
  • Expanding operating hours 24/7 availability
  • Reducing wait times better supply positioning

Demand Variability Challenges

Uber identified significant demand variability challenges in Austin:

  • Monday demand is 45% of Saturday demand
  • Daily trough-to-peak demand variation is 5x

This variability presents both challenges and opportunities:

  • Challenge:AVs must be efficiently deployed across variable demand
  • Opportunity:Uber's matching algorithms excel at this optimization
  • Implication:Hybrid model provides flexibility that pure AV fleets lack

The ability to supplement AV capacity with human drivers during peak demand periods is a critical advantage of the hybrid model. Pure AV operators e.g., Waymo face higher volatility risk as they scale.

Geographic and Demographic Considerations

Uber's AV strategy considers diverse market characteristics:

Market Diversity:

  • US Top 20 cities represent only 30% of Gross Bookings
  • US Top 20 cities represent only 25% of profits
  • NYC represents 10%+ of US trips, but 75% of NYC trips stay in outer boroughs
  • 40% of US riders travel outside their home city

These data points suggest:

  • Profit pools are diverse and fragmented no single market dominates
  • Suburban opportunities exist beyond dense urban cores
  • Travel network effects enable national scaling
  • Geographic diversification reduces concentration risk

Demographic Variability:

San Francisco demographic characteristics from supplemental data:

  • Population density: 2x LA, 4x US top 50 average
  • Median income: 2x LA
  • Miles per trip: 60% fewer than LA

This suggests AV deployment success in San Francisco may not generalize:

  • High density supports high trip density efficient AV utilization
  • High income enables premium pricing better unit economics
  • Shorter trips reduce cost per mile favorable for current AV tech

Austin and Atlanta likely have different demographics lower density, lower income, longer trips, suggesting Uber's AV partners have made technology advances enabling broader deployment.
