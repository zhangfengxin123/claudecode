Executive Summary

The University of Nottingham's 2023/24 financial performance presents a mixed picture of significant achievements alongside notable challenges. The university reported a total income of £849.4 million up 3.2% year-on-year but faced its first adjusted deficit in recent years at £17.0 million, compared to a £3.0 million surplus in 2022/23. This deficit stems primarily from a 5.0% decline in student numbers combined with high inflationary pressures that outpaced income growth.

Despite these challenges, the university demonstrated remarkable resilience in key strategic areas. Research income reached a record £141.6 million up 9.3%, international postgraduate taught recruitment remained stable, and the USS pension scheme moved from deficit to surplus, resulting in a £243.6m actuarial gain. The university maintained its A+ credit rating and has £80m of undrawn credit facilities available, providing financial flexibility for future operations.

Cash Flow and Liquidity Analysis

Cash from operations declined from £27.2m in 2022/23 to £4.9m in 2023/24, reflecting the operating deficit. The university has responded by:

  • Using cash reserves to bridge the gap £54.5m reduction in cash position
  • Maintaining £80m undrawn revolving credit facility

Liquidity Position

At year-end 2023/24:

  • Cash: £69.9m
  • Undrawn credit facilities: £80m
  • Total available liquidity: £149.9m

This provides approximately 2.1 months of operating costs based on current expenditure levels, which is within acceptable ranges for UK higher education institutions.

The University of Nottingham's 2023/24 financial performance reveals a university in transition. While facing short-term challenges from declining student numbers and inflationary pressures, the institution maintains strong fundamentals:

Strengths:

  • Record research income demonstrating academic excellence
  • Strong international PGT recruitment showing global competitiveness
  • USS pension gain providing long-term financial relief
  • A+ credit rating indicating strong financial standing

Challenges:

  • Student recruitment shortfall requiring strategic response
  • Staff cost inflation creating structural cost pressures
  • Reduced cash position requiring liquidity management
  • Adjusted deficit indicating need for operational efficiency
