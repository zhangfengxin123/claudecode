Executive Summary

The Federal Funds Rate has undergone one of the most dramatic policy transformations in modern economic history between 2021-2026. This analysis reveals a remarkable journey from near-zero rates 0.05% in early 2021 to peak levels 5.33% by mid-2023, followed by an emerging easing cycle in 2025. Our examination of 1,827 daily observations shows 68 distinct rate changes, with significant implications for gold prices, inflation dynamics, and investment strategy.

Key Findings:

  • Rate Volatility: 68 rate changes over 5 years, with the most aggressive hiking cycle in four decades
  • Peak Rate: 5.33% reached in July 2023 - the highest since 2007
  • Current Trend: Easing phase beginning in late 2024/early 2025
  • Gold Correlation: Inverse relationship with interest rates, though moderated by inflation expectations

2.2 Key Insights by Period

2021: Pandemic Recovery Mode

The Fed maintained historically low rates averaging 0.08%, supporting economic recovery from COVID-19. Gold prices remained elevated as real interest rates were negative.

2022: Inflation Response

The most dramatic rate increases occurred in 2022:

  • Start: 0.07% January
  • End: 4.33% December
  • Change: +4.26 percentage points
  • Impact: Gold price volatility increased as real rates turned positive

2023: Peak Policy Rates

Rates peaked at 5.33% in July 2023, the highest level since the 2007-2008 financial crisis. This represented a 52x increase from the 2021 trough.

2024: "Higher for Longer"

Despite inflation moderating, the Fed maintained rates near peak levels, adopting a cautious approach to ensure inflation returned to 2% target.

2025-2026: Easing Cycle Begins

Following three consecutive rate cuts in late 2024 September, November, December, the easing cycle continued into 2025, with projections for further reductions.

5. Gold Price Correlation Analysis

5.1 Theoretical Relationship

Inverse Correlation Mechanism

Economic Theory:

1. Opportunity Cost: Higher interest rates increase the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding assets like gold

2. Real Interest Rates: Gold is sensitive to real rates nominal rates minus inflation

3. Currency Impact: Higher rates strengthen USD, making gold more expensive for foreign buyers

4. Inflation Hedge: Gold preserves value during inflation when real rates are negative
