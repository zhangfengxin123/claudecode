US Interest Rate Analysis: SOFR and Economic Impact

2021-2025

Executive Summary

This report analyzes the Secured Overnight Financing Rate SOFR 30-day average alongside US Nominal GDP to understand the relationship between Federal Reserve monetary policy and economic output. The analysis reveals a strong negative correlation of -0.7961between SOFR rates and GDP growth, highlighting the Fed's inflation-fighting mandate and its impact on economic growth.

Key findings:

  • SOFR rates surged from near-zero levels 0.04% in 2021 to multi-decade highs 5.35% in 2024
  • The rapid rate hikes in 2022-2023 represent the most aggressive monetary tightening cycle since the 1980s
  • US GDP shows remarkable resilience despite elevated interest rates
  • The Fed appears to be achieving a "soft landing" with inflation cooling while growth remains positive

SOFR-GDP Relationship Analysis

Correlation Analysis

sofr_gdp_correlation.json

Key Finding:

-0.7961

This correlation suggests:

1. Higher rates coincide with slower growth- The Fed's rate hikes are working as intended to cool economic activity and reduce inflationary pressures.

2. Policy lag effects- The timing mismatch between policy implementation and economic impact explains the correlation pattern.

3. Soft landing achievement- Despite the strong negative relationship, growth remains positive, suggesting the Fed may achieve its soft landing goal.

Economic Interpretation

The negative correlation reflects standard monetary transmission:

  • Higher rates→ Higher borrowing costs→ Reduced consumption and investment→ Slower GDP growth
  • Lower rates→ Cheaper credit→ Increased spending→ Faster GDP growth

Monetary Policy Timeline and Context

Phase 1: Post-COVID Stimulus

2021

  • SOFR averaged 0.04% essentially zero
  • Fed maintained ultra-loose policy
  • GDP grew from 5,670B to 6,204B 9.4%
  • Policy goal: Support recovery from pandemic recession

Phase 2: Rapid Tightening

2022-2023

  • SOFR surged from 0.05% to 5.34%
  • Fed raised rates at fastest pace since Volcker era
  • Response to 40-year high inflation
  • GDP slowed but remained positive
  • Policy goal: Anchor inflation expectations

Phase 3: Rate Plateau

2024

  • SOFR averaged 5.19% historic peak
  • Fed paused rate hikes to assess impact
  • GDP demonstrated resilience
  • Policy goal: Evaluate transmission effects

Phase 4: Policy Pivot

2025

  • SOFR averaged 4.28% gradual decline
  • Fed begins rate cuts
  • GDP maintains growth trajectory
  • Policy goal: Normalize policy without recession

Conclusion

The analysis reveals a well-functioning monetary transmission mechanism, with SOFR rates demonstrating a strong negative relationship with GDP growth -0.7961. The Federal Reserve's aggressive tightening cycle 2022-2024 appears to have achieved its primary objective of cooling inflationary pressures without inducing a recession—a rare "soft landing" achievement.

Looking ahead, the gradual rate cuts expected in 2025-2026 should support continued economic growth while maintaining price stability. The US economy's resilience throughout this cycle underscores its structural strength and the effectiveness of independent monetary policy.
