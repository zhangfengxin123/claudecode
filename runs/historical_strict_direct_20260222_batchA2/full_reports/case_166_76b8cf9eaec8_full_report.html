Cognitive Performance Analysis in Social Decision-Making Tasks

Executive Summary

This report presents a comprehensive analysis of experimental data from a dot enumeration task involving 41 participants across 120 trials each. The study examines task performance, confidence calibration, partner influence, and underlying cognitive processes modeled through drift diffusion parameters.

Key Findings:

  • Overall Accuracy: 65.4% SD = 2.2% with significant variability across conditions
  • Partner Agreement: Strong positive correlation r = 0.737 with task accuracy, suggesting social influence
  • Optimal Difficulty: 57-64 dots range shows highest performance 71-83% accuracy
  • Confidence Calibration: Well-calibrated, with higher confidence ratings predicting higher accuracy
  • Learning Effects: Minimal improvement across trial quartiles p = 0.703

12. Key Insights and Recommendations

12.1 Primary Findings

1. Social Influence Dominates: Partner agreement is the strongest predictor of task accuracy r = 0.737, suggesting social information heavily influences decision-making.

2. Well-Calibrated Confidence: Participants show good metacognitive awareness, with higher confidence predicting higher accuracy.

3. Minimal Learning Effects: While response times decrease with practice speed-up, accuracy remains stable, indicating the task was well-practiced or plateaued early.

4. Counter-Intuitive Difficulty Effect: Higher dot counts 57-64 are associated with higher accuracy, suggesting perceptual information quality outweighs cognitive load.

12.2 Practical Implications

  • Task Design: Consider how social information is presented, as it appears to override individual cognitive processing
  • Training: Focus on decision quality rather than speed, as practice primarily improves speed
  • Assessment: Confidence ratings provide valid information about decision quality

12.3 Limitations

1. DDM Model Fit: Traditional drift diffusion parameters show limited explanatory power for this task

2. Condition Manipulation: Small differences between conditions suggest weak experimental manipulation

3. Trial Balance: Limited trials at extreme dot counts 61-64 reduce reliability of those estimates

12.4 Future Directions

1. Model Development: Consider alternative cognitive models that incorporate social influence parameters

2. Manipulation Check: Verify the effectiveness of condition manipulations

3. Individual Differences: Explore factors explaining variance between subjects e.g., social conformity traits

13. Conclusion

This comprehensive analysis reveals a task where social information partner agreement overwhelmingly drives decision-making, with individual cognitive processes playing a secondary role. The findings challenge traditional models of perceptual decision-making by highlighting the primacy of social influence in this experimental paradigm.

The study demonstrates excellent experimental control balanced conditions, minimal missing data, well-calibrated confidence judgments but suggests the need for cognitive models that explicitly incorporate social influence parameters rather than treating social information as noise.
