Executive Summary

This comprehensive analysis examines 452 sleep records to identify the key factors influencing sleep efficiency. Our analysis reveals that deep sleep duration is the strongest predictorof sleep efficiency with a correlation coefficient of 0.79. Additionally, we discovered significant differences in sleep quality between smokers and non-smokers, with non-smokers achieving 82% efficiency compared to 73% for smokers.

Key Findings:

  • Deep sleep accounts for 62.4% of the variation in sleep efficiency
  • Non-smokers experience 9 percentage points higher sleep efficiency
  • Gender shows minimal impact on sleep quality
  • Age distribution is balanced mean: 40.3 years, range: 20-65

Limitations & Future Research

Study Limitations

1. Cross-sectional data: Cannot establish causality

Future Research Directions

Conclusion

This analysis of 452 sleep records identifies deep sleep durationas the most critical factor influencing sleep efficiency, followed by smoking status. The strong correlation r = 0.79 between deep sleep and efficiency suggests that interventions targeting deep sleep improvement could yield substantial benefits.

The significant difference in sleep quality between smokers and non-smokers provides compelling evidence for the sleep-related benefits of smoking cessation. Our regression model explains 72.4% of the variation in sleep efficiency, providing a robust framework for predicting and improving sleep quality.
