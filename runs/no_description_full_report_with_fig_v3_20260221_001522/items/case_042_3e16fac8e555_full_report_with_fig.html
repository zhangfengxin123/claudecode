Employee Attrition Analysis: Key Drivers and Retention Strategies

Executive Summary

This report presents a comprehensive analysis of employee attrition across an organization with 10,000 employees. The overall attrition rate stands at 9.03%, which is below the industry average of 10-15%, indicating relatively healthy retention. However, several critical risk factors and departmental variations require attention.

Methodology

This analysis employed a multi-method approach combining exploratory data analysis, statistical modeling, and machine learning techniques:

1. Data Cleaning: Processed 10,000 employee records with no missing values or duplicates

2. Exploratory Analysis: Examined attrition patterns across 14 employee attributes

3. Correlation Analysis: Calculated Pearson correlations between attrition and all features

4. Logistic Regression: Built a predictive model with 90.95% overall accuracy ROC AUC: 0.66

5. Feature Importance: Identified top risk factors using model coefficients

The dataset includes comprehensive employee information spanning demographics, career progression, satisfaction metrics, and work conditions, enabling a holistic view of attrition drivers.

Conclusion

This comprehensive analysis of employee attrition reveals that while the organization's 9.03% attrition rate is below industry average, significant opportunities exist to improve retention. The three most critical drivers—job satisfaction, work-life balance, and overtime—represent actionable levers that can deliver substantial improvements.

The analysis demonstrates that attrition is not uniform across the organization. Entry-level employees, those in Engineering and Sales departments, and employees with low satisfaction scores face disproportionate attrition risk. Targeted interventions addressing these specific segments will yield the highest return on investment.
