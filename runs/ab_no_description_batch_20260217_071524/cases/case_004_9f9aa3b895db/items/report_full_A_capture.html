Economic Indicators Analysis: Fed Funds Rate, Import Prices & NASDAQ Market Performance

2021-2025

Executive Summary

This analysis examines the relationship between Federal Reserve monetary policy as reflected in the Federal Funds Rate, international trade dynamics Import Price Index, and equity market performance NASDAQ Composite Index from February 2021 to December 2025. During this period, the U.S. economy experienced one of the most aggressive monetary tightening cycles in history, with the Fed Funds Rate rising from near-zero levels 0.06% to a 23-year high of 5.33%.

Key Findings:

  • The NASDAQ Composite showed remarkable resilience despite aggressive monetary tightening, delivering average monthly returns of 1.11% during the analysis period
  • Federal Funds Rate volatility was extreme, with monthly changes as large as 0.75 percentage points
  • Market returns were highest during the low-rate environment near-zero rates but remained positive even as rates climbed above 5%
  • The correlation between Fed Funds Rate and NASDAQ monthly returns was weak 0.19, challenging conventional wisdom about rate hikes immediately crushing equity markets

Data Overview and Methodology

分析问题
  • What data sources were used for this analysis?
  • How was the daily NASDAQ data transformed to match monthly frequency?

This analysis integrates three key economic indicators:

Data Sources

1. Federal Funds Rate FEDFUNDS: Monthly data from 1954-2026, representing the interest rate at which depository institutions lend reserve balances to other depository institutions overnight

2. Import Price Index IR: Monthly data from 1982-2025, measuring changes in the prices of goods purchased from abroad

3. NASDAQ Composite Index NASDAQCOM: Daily data from 2021-2026, representing the performance of over 3,000 technology and growth-oriented stocks

Data Processing

The datasets were merged based on the common observation period February 2021 to December 2025, resulting in 58 monthly observations. Daily NASDAQ data was aggregated to monthly frequency using the following metrics:

  • Monthly Mean: Average index level for the month
  • Monthly Return: Percentage change from first to last trading day
  • Standard Deviation: Intra-month volatility measure

All data cleaning procedures removed null values and ensured proper date alignment across datasets.

Federal Funds Rate Dynamics

The Federal Funds Rate experienced unprecedented volatility during the 2021-2025 period, reflecting the Federal Reserve's aggressive response to post-pandemic inflation.

Rate Trajectory

  • Starting Point Feb 2021: 0.06% - near-zero levels maintained during COVID-19 pandemic
  • Ending Point Dec 2025: 5.33% - highest level since 2001
  • Peak Rate: 5.33% achieved in December 2023 and maintained through much of 2024-2025
  • Total Increase: 5.27 percentage points over 58 months

Monthly Rate Changes

The distribution of monthly rate changes reveals the aggressive nature of the tightening cycle:

  • Average Monthly Change: +0.09 percentage points
  • Maximum Monthly Increase: +0.75 percentage points March 2022
  • Maximum Monthly Decrease: -0.25 percentage points March 2024
  • Rate Increase Periods: 36 of 58 months 62%
  • Rate Decrease/Unchanged Periods: 22 of 58 months 38%

The most significant rate hikes occurred in 2022, with five consecutive months of 0.50-0.75 percentage point increases from March to July 2022, marking the fastest tightening cycle since the early 1980s.

Key Insights

1. Fed Funds Rate - NASDAQ Return Paradox: The weak positive correlation 0.19 between Fed Funds Rate levels and NASDAQ returns challenges conventional wisdom. This suggests that:

  • Markets may have already priced in rate expectations
  • Technology companies in the NASDAQ showed resilience through strong earnings growth
  • Rate hikes were often accompanied by strong economic growth signals that supported equity valuations

2. Inflation Transmission Mechanism: The strong correlation 0.71 between Fed Funds Rate and Import Price Index demonstrates how the Federal Reserve responds to imported inflation pressures. When import prices rise, the Fed typically tightens policy to combat domestic inflation.

3. Economic Growth Proxy: The very strong correlation 0.87 between NASDAQ levels and Import Price Index suggests both variables reflect underlying economic growth. As the economy expands, demand for imports increases pushing up prices and technology company earnings grow supporting higher equity prices.

Policy Response Mechanism

The strong positive correlation 0.71 between Import Price Index and Fed Funds Rate reveals the Fed's inflation-fighting strategy:

1. Import Price Surge 2021-2022: As supply chain disruptions and commodity price increases drove import prices higher, the Fed began raising rates in early 2022

2. Policy Aggressiveness: The sharpest rate hikes corresponded with periods of highest import price inflation, particularly Q2 2022 when rates rose by 0.75 percentage points per month

3. Import Price Stabilization 2023-2024: As import prices began moderating, the Fed shifted to a "higher for longer" stance rather than continued aggressive hikes

4. Inflation Persistence: Despite import price declines in 2023, the Fed maintained high rates due to sticky core inflation measures

Implications for Investors and Policymakers

分析问题
  • What should investors consider when positioning portfolios in different rate environments?
  • What lessons can policymakers draw from this period?

Investor Insights

1. Don't Fear Rate Hikes Blindly: The data shows that equity markets can perform well even in high-rate environments, particularly when rate hikes are accompanied by economic growth

2. Focus on Earnings Fundamentals: The NASDAQ's resilience suggests that companies with strong earnings growth can outperform even when monetary conditions tighten

3. Volatility is Rate-Dependent: As shown in Chart 2, volatility increases significantly when rates exceed 3%, suggesting investors should adjust risk management strategies accordingly

4. Timing is Difficult: The weak correlation between rate changes and monthly returns suggests trying to time the market based on monetary policy announcements is challenging

5. Long-Term Perspective Matters: Despite significant rate hikes, the NASDAQ delivered substantial cumulative returns over the 58-month period

Policy Implications

1. Market Resilience is Robust: The financial system demonstrated ability to absorb rapid monetary tightening without systemic failure

2. Communication is Critical: The Fed's transparent signaling of rate hikes likely helped markets adjust expectations in advance

3. Import Price Transmission is Significant: The strong correlation between import prices and policy rates suggests the Fed should carefully monitor imported inflation pressures

4. Lagged Effects are Important: The fact that markets performed well in high-rate environments suggests policy operates with significant lags, and immediate market reactions may not reflect long-term economic fundamentals

Limitations and Considerations

分析问题
  • What are the limitations of this analysis?
  • What additional factors could influence these relationships?

Methodological Limitations

1. Correlation ≠ Causation: The observed relationships do not prove that Fed policy caused market movements or vice versa

2. Short Time Period: 58 months may not capture full business cycle dynamics

3. Omitted Variables: Other factors corporate earnings, geopolitical events, fiscal policy are not explicitly modeled

4. Aggregation Bias: Monthly aggregation may obscure important intra-month dynamics

External Factors Not Captured

  • Corporate earnings growth and revisions
  • Geopolitical events Russia-Ukraine conflict, Middle East tensions
  • Fiscal policy changes American Rescue Plan, Infrastructure Investment Act
  • Sector-specific developments AI boom, semiconductor shortages
  • Global monetary policy coordination

Conclusion

This comprehensive analysis of Federal Funds Rate, Import Price Index, and NASDAQ Composite performance from 2021-2025 reveals several counterintuitive insights that challenge traditional economic assumptions.

Key Takeaways:

1. Equity markets showed remarkable resilienceduring one of the most aggressive monetary tightening cycles in history, with the NASDAQ delivering average monthly returns of 1.11%

2. The relationship between interest rates and equity returns is non-linear and complex, with weak correlation suggesting many other factors drive market performance

3. High-rate environments can support positive returnsif accompanied by economic growth and strong corporate fundamentals

4. Import price inflation was a key driver of monetary policy, with the Fed responding aggressively to imported inflation pressures in 2021-2022

5. Market volatility increases with interest rate levels, requiring investors to adjust risk management strategies in higher-rate environments

The analysis suggests that investors should avoid simplistic assumptions about interest rate impacts on equity markets and instead focus on fundamental economic and corporate performance drivers. Similarly, policymakers should recognize that markets can adapt to changing monetary conditions, particularly when policy shifts are well-communicated and gradual.

For future research, incorporating additional variables corporate earnings, sector performance, international capital flows and extending the time period would provide deeper insights into these complex relationships.
