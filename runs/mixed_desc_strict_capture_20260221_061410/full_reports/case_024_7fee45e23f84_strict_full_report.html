This comprehensive analysis reveals a market in deep contraction, characterized by accelerating price declines, a developer crisis of unprecedented scale, and significant economic impacts across multiple sectors.

Key findings include:

  • Price Decline Acceleration: National new home prices fell 2.7% year-over-year in December 2025, the steepest decline since July 2025, with prices now down 30 months in a row

Introduction

China's housing market has undergone a dramatic transformation since 2021, shifting from a 20-year boom fueled by urbanization and rising household incomes to a prolonged downturn marked by declining prices, developer defaults, and policy uncertainty. This report analyzes the comprehensive dataset spanning 2023-2025, examining price trends across 70 major cities, the economic impact of the property sector decline, and the effectiveness of policy interventions.

The data reveals a market that has been searching for a bottom for nearly three years, with December 2025 marking the 30th consecutive month of price declines. This downturn represents a fundamental shift in China's economic structure, as property has historically accounted for 20-25% of total fixed-asset investment and represented 70% of urban household assets.

This analysis examines the multi-dimensional nature of the crisis through four key lenses: price trends and regional variations, the developer credit crisis, macroeconomic impacts, and policy responses and outlook.

National Price Decline Trajectory

China's new home prices across 70 cities have experienced a systematic and accelerating decline since 2023, with no signs of reversal through December 2025. The average YoY decline has deepened from -1.15% in 2023 to -2.30% in 2024 and -2.37% in 2025, indicating that while the rate of acceleration has slowed, prices continue to fall at an elevated pace.

The decline trajectory reveals three distinct phases:

Phase 1 2023: Initial decline averaging -1.15% YoY, with accelerating deterioration throughout the year as market sentiment shifted and financing conditions tightened for developers. The acceleration during this period averaged -0.164 percentage points per month, reflecting a rapid deterioration in market conditions.

Phase 2 2024: Deepening decline averaging -2.30% YoY, with the market searching for a bottom but finding limited support despite policy interventions. Acceleration slowed to -0.042 percentage points per month, suggesting some stabilization in the rate of decline.

Phase 3 2025: Sustained decline averaging -2.37% YoY, with the steepest monthly decline of -2.7% occurring in December 2025. The rate of acceleration further slowed to -0.017 percentage points per month, indicating the market may be approaching a stabilization phase, albeit at a lower price level.

Default Cascade

The developer crisis has reached unprecedented proportions, with 85 developers defaulting by 2025, up from 52 in 2023, 68 in 2024, and just 5 in 2019. This represents a 17-fold increase in defaults since the pre-crisis period and reflects the severe stress in the property sector's financing model.

The continued increase through 2025 suggests that the credit cycle has yet to reach its trough.

Financing Model Collapse

The developer crisis reflects a fundamental breakdown in China's traditional property financing model. Developers who relied on aggressive expansion fueled by easy credit found themselves acutely vulnerable when market sentiment shifted and financing tightened. Many had trouble accessing bank loans and instead tapped the bond market, particularly US dollar-denominated debt with yields as high as 10%.

Global bondholders, attracted by these lucrative returns, ended up with significant losses from the defaults, creating international contagion risk and constraining future access to capital markets for Chinese property developers.

The financing model breakdown has several key characteristics:

  • Overleveraging: Many developers borrowed aggressively during the boom period, with debt-to-equity ratios exceeding 100%
  • Short-term liabilities: Heavy reliance on rolling short-term debt to fund long-term projects, creating liquidity risk
  • Offshore debt: Significant US dollar-denominated debt exposure, creating currency risk and regulatory challenges
  • Pre-sale dependency: Reliance on pre-sale proceeds to fund ongoing construction, creating a Ponzi-like structure when sales decline

GDP Drag

The property downturn has become a significant drag on China's economic growth. Goldman Sachs estimates that the property crisis has reduced annual real GDP growth by approximately 2 percentage points in 2024 and 2025, with the drag expected to narrow to 0.5 percentage points annually in coming years.

The GDP impact operates through multiple channels:

  • Direct investment: Property development accounts for 20-25% of total fixed-asset investment, making it a major contributor to capital formation
  • Construction activity: Declines in new construction reduce demand for materials, equipment, and labor
  • Financial services: Reduced mortgage originations and property transactions depress income for banks, insurers, and brokerages
  • Consumer spending: Negative wealth effects from falling property values reduce household consumption

Despite this drag, China has maintained a 4-5% GDP growth rate through 2025, reflecting the success of policy efforts to pivot toward technology innovation, high-end manufacturing, green transition, and domestic demand stimulation the "dual circulation" strategy.

Wealth Effect and Consumer Behavior

The negative wealth effect from property has significantly impacted consumer sentiment. Residential property constitutes 70% of urban household assets, and falling housing prices have made people feel poorer. As a result, people choose to spend less and save more.

In the past five years, household deposits in banks have nearly doubled, indicating a shift from property speculation to risk-averse savings behavior. This has several implications:

  • Consumption contraction: Reduced consumer spending slows economic rebalancing toward domestic consumption
  • Deflationary pressure: Increased savings and reduced spending contribute to deflationary risks
  • Investment reallocation: Capital is flowing from property to other assets, including domestic equities
  • Policy challenge: Wealth effect dynamics create resistance to monetary and fiscal stimulus

Banking System Stability

Despite the severe property downturn, China's banking system remains stable with no bank failures reported through 2025. This stability reflects several structural factors:

Conservative Lending Standards:

  • Mortgage non-performing loans remain below 1% at major Chinese banks
  • Down payments traditionally required 30%+ for first homes, 40-50% for second homes
  • Even after May 2024 easing, minimum down payments remain 20% for first homes, 30-35% for second homes
  • No subprime mortgage lending in China

Substantial Equity Buffers:

  • High down payment requirements create substantial home equity cushioning against price declines
  • Chinese households are not overly leveraged relative to Western standards
  • Conservative collateral requirements ensure most loans are backed by substantial underlying assets

Regulatory Resilience:

  • Banks maintain ample loan-loss reserves as mandated by regulators
  • Stress testing and capital adequacy requirements provide additional protection
  • Government has indicated willingness to support the banking system if needed

The stability of the banking system represents a critical difference from the 2008 US housing crisis, where subprime lending and complex securitization amplified financial contagion.

  • Relaxation of home purchase restrictions in secondary cities
  • Encouragement for banks to provide reasonable financing to qualified developers
  • Acceleration of urban renewal projects

2025: Intensified Support

Successes:

  • Affordability improvement: Mortgage rates reduced from 4.9% 2019 to 3.4% 2025

Challenges:

  • Investment contraction: Property investment still falling -17.2% YoY December 2025
  • Developer crisis: Defaults continuing to increase 85 in 2025 vs 68 in 2024
  • Consumer confidence: Wealth effect continues to depress consumption

The limited effectiveness of policy interventions reflects structural challenges:

  • Oversupply: Years of overbuilding have created substantial inventory
  • Demographic shifts: Population aging and urbanization slowdown reduce structural demand
  • Affordability limits: High price-to-income ratios constrain new buyer demand
  • Speculative retreat: Exit of speculative investors reduces marginal demand

Conclusions and Strategic Implications

Key Takeaways

1. Prolonged Downturn: China's housing market is experiencing its most severe and prolonged downturn in decades, with 30 consecutive months of price declines through December 2025. The market shows no signs of imminent recovery, with declines averaging -2.4% YoY in 2025.

2. Regional Divergence: Significant variation across cities, with Tier 1 cities averaging -1.7% decline, Tier 2 cities -2.7%, and Tier 3 cities -3.4%. Shanghai remains the outlier with +4.8% growth, while Guangzhou and Shenzhen face severe declines.

3. Developer Crisis: 85 developer defaults by 2025 represent a 17-fold increase from 2019, reflecting a fundamental breakdown in the property financing model. The default rate shows no signs of peaking, suggesting continued stress ahead.

  • Construction materials: Overcapacity persists, but structural demand remains at reduced levels

Final Assessment

China's housing market downturn represents a structural shift rather than a cyclical downturn. The 20-year boom fueled by urbanization and household income growth has run its course, and the market is transitioning to a new equilibrium characterized by lower but more sustainable demand.

The policy response has successfully prevented systemic financial crisis and maintained overall economic stability. However, reversing the price decline remains challenging given the depth of oversupply, reduced speculative demand, and demographic headwinds.

Investors and stakeholders should prepare for an extended adjustment period 3-5 years rather than expecting a quick recovery. Opportunities exist for those with strong balance sheets and patient capital, but volatility and downside risks remain elevated in the near term.

The long-term outlook suggests a more mature, stable, and less speculative property market that plays a smaller but more sustainable role in China's economic structure. This transition, while painful in the short term, may contribute to higher-quality, more sustainable economic growth over the long term.
