Impact Analysis: Repeated Carrier Contacts for Same Shipment

Executive Summary

This analysis examines the impact of contacting the same carrier multiple times for the same shipment. The data reveals significant inefficiencies in current contact practices, with 19.68% of all shipment-carrier pairsreceiving multiple contact attempts. This redundancy results in 920 wasted contacts486 emails and 434 phone calls, representing a 20.41% overall waste rate.

Key Findings:

  • 360 out of 1,829 shipment-carrier pairs 19.68% were contacted multiple times
  • Average of 1.31 contact attempts per pair
  • 21.94% email waste and 18.93% phone call waste
  • Extreme cases: Some pairs contacted up to 16 times with minimal unique contacts
  • Total potential savings: 920 redundant contacts could be eliminated

Data Overview

The dataset contains 1,829 unique shipment-carrier pairs with detailed tracking of contact attempts through email and phone channels. Each record tracks the total number of contact attempts TOTALROWS, email attempts EMAILCOUNT, unique email contacts UNIQUEEMAILCOUNT, phone attempts PHONECOUNT, and unique phone contacts UNIQUEPHONECOUNT.

Root Cause Analysis

Potential Causes of Multiple Contacts

1. Lack of Real-Time Deduplication

Contact systems may not check for recent attempts before initiating new contacts

Parallel systems may not communicate effectively

2. Manual Override Issues

Sales representatives may bypass automated systems

Lack of visibility into previous contact attempts

3. Systemic Process Flaws

Multiple teams or departments contacting independently

Absence of centralized contact history

4. Data Quality Issues

Inconsistent carrier identification leading to duplicate records

Merged shipment data creating duplicate contact triggers

5. Retry Logic Without Smart Limits

Automated retry mechanisms may not respect uniqueness constraints

Lack of intelligent cooldown periods between attempts
