I. Trust
Read the data correctly: reciprocal vs round-robin pairing

Trust-game data is round robin, not reciprocal.

Each row ties together three roles/IDs: Player’s Sender → Player → Player’s Receiver.

A player’s score aggregates what happened when someone sent to them and when they sent to someone else.

Trust Game (TR)

Point-maximizer benchmark strategies for Stage 1 (send) and Stage 2 (return).

Interpretations:

Stage 1 sent amount ≈ trust

Stage 2 returned share ≈ trustworthiness

Describe distributions; assess whether trust and trustworthiness “correlate” in the everyday sense (no coefficients).

Use Random Row Picker: sample ~12 players and use rationales to refine what Stage 1/2 measure (beyond “trust/trustworthiness”).

Demographics benchmark: compare trust/trustworthiness by gender and by race/ethnicity.

Link survey attitudes to behavior:

Diversity Survey “most people can be trusted…” vs actual TR behavior.

Did the class anticipate the realized trustworthiness distribution?

TR vs Trust with Gender + Race/Ethnicity (GRE)

Two approaches:

Across games: does adding identity info change trust/trustworthiness vs baseline TR?

Within GRE: compare encounters by gender pairing (MM/MF/FM/FF) and by race/ethnicity combinations.

Note measurement issue: gameplay uses free-form identity; dataset uses radio-button categories (thin statistical simplification of social reality).

Test Robert Putnam’s homogeneity hypothesis:

Are homogeneous pairs more trusting/trustworthy than heterogeneous pairs?

Which matters more: gender homogeneity or race/ethnicity homogeneity?

Identify highest/lowest trust & trustworthiness combinations.

Compare to Chaim Fershtman & Uri Gneezy (gender × race/ethnicity result) and to international evidence (Croson/Buchan/Dawes studies).

Trust with Hogwarts Houses (HH)

Pairing is within-house (neighborhood-like sorting), unlike anonymous heterogeneous TR (city-like mixing).

Use TR as benchmark: do same-house pairs show higher trust/trustworthiness?

Confounds to keep explicit:

HH reveals Pen Names + shared house membership; TR is anonymous and identity-blind.

So differences may reflect familiarity + in-group identity, not just “sorting.”
