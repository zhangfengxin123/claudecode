This analysis explores the TMDB Movies Dataset containing 20,118 moviesreleased between 1902 and 2025. The dataset provides rich insights into movie ratings, genres, languages, popularity metrics, and temporal trends across more than a century of cinema.

  • Vintage advantage: Older movies pre-1980s tend to have higher average ratings
  • Decline through 1990s: Average ratings decreased through the late 20th century
  • Stabilization: 2000s-2020s show relatively stable average ratings around 6.0-6.5
  • Recent dip: Slight decrease in average ratings in the 2020s

Possible Explanations:

Selection Bias:

  • Only the best older movies survive in cultural memory
  • Bad old movies are forgotten, while all new movies are documented
  • This creates a "survivor bias" favoring older classics

Changing Standards:

  • Audiences today have access to more content, making them more critical
  • Increased competition raises expectations
  • Modern audiences are exposed to global cinema, increasing comparative standards

Rating Inflation/Deflation:

  • Older movies may benefit from nostalgic viewing
  • Newer movies face immediate scrutiny from diverse global audiences
  • Rating platforms may have different user demographics over time

Data Quality:

  • Older movies in the dataset are likely the most famous/acclaimed
  • Newer movies include everything released, creating an unfair comparison

Recommendations for Movie Analysis

For Researchers:

  • Consider vote count when interpreting ratings minimum threshold: 500-1,000 votes
  • Account for language bias when making global generalizations
  • Use decade-based analysis to control for temporal trends
  • Examine genre-specific patterns rather than aggregate metrics

For Content Creators:

  • Drama and Comedy are the most common genres for a reason
  • High ratings drive organic discovery and engagement
  • Action/Adventure achieve highest popularity, even with moderate ratings
  • International markets offer significant opportunities

For Platforms:

  • Weight ratings by vote count in recommendation algorithms
  • Consider language preferences in personalization
  • Promote high-rated, low-vote movies to help quality films find audiences
  • Use genre hybridization to increase content diversity

Data Limitations

Survivor Bias:

  • Older movies in dataset likely represent only the best from each era
  • Bad old movies are forgotten, while all new movies are documented
  • This creates unfair advantages for older films in rating comparisons

Vote Count Bias:

  • Newer movies have more potential voters larger global online audience
  • Movies from non-English speaking regions may have fewer votes due to language barriers
  • Popular franchises benefit from fan voting regardless of quality

Platform Bias:

  • TMDB users may not represent general movie-going audiences
  • Sci-Fi/Fantasy and Action may be overrepresented among online communities
  • Certain regions may have better documentation than others
