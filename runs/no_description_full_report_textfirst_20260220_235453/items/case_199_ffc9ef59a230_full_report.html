Federal Reserve Policy Impact on US Tech Software Sector: A Data-Driven Analysis

Executive Summary

This analysis examines the relationship between Federal Reserve monetary policy and the US technology software sector from January 2021 through January 2026. The data reveals a strong negative correlation of -0.806between the Federal Funds Rate and the US Tech Software Development Index, indicating that as interest rates rose, the tech sector experienced significant valuation declines.

Key Findings:

  • The tech index peaked at 233.93 in February 2022when the Fed rate was 0.08% near-zero
  • As the Fed raised rates from 0.08% to 5.33% July 2023, the tech index declined by 70.5%
  • The forecast suggests a continued easing cycle, with rates projected to fall to 3.64% by January 2026
  • Tech index forecasts indicate continued challenges, with a projected value of 69.06 in January 2026

This analysis provides actionable insights for investors, policymakers, and tech sector stakeholders on the profound impact of monetary policy on technology sector valuations.

Limitations & Caveats

Data Limitations

1. Forecast Uncertainty:The forecast data 2025-2026 relies on projections that may prove inaccurate

2. Single Index Analysis:The US Tech Software Development Index may not fully represent the entire tech ecosystem

3. Correlation vs. Causation:While the relationship is strong, other factors earnings, market cycles also influence tech performance

4. Time Period Limited:Analysis covers a specific period 2021-2026 that may not represent all economic cycles

Model Limitations

1. Linear Assumption:The regression model assumes a linear relationship, which may not hold at extreme rate levels

2. Lag Effects:The impact of rate changes may take 6-18 months to fully materialize in markets

3. Non-Fed Factors:The model does not account for sector-specific fundamentals, competitive dynamics, or technological innovation

Contextual Limitations

1. COVID Anomalies:The 2021-2022 period includes pandemic-related distortions

2. Geopolitical Events:Wars, trade policies, and other global factors not captured in the model

3. Regulatory Changes:Antitrust actions and tech regulations not considered in the analysis
