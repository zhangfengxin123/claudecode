CAP Analysis Report 2023-2025: Quality Management Insights

Executive Summary

This report provides a comprehensive analysis of 37 Corrective Action Plans CAPs recorded between February 2023 and September 2025. The analysis reveals key trends, cost patterns, supplier performance, and closure efficiency metrics that can inform strategic quality management decisions.

Key Findings:

  • Total CAP Costs: €869,249.88 average: €23,493 per CAP
  • Primary CAP Type: Claims account for 54% of all CAPs 20 out of 37
  • Top Cost Drivers: Mitsubishi €533,696, Inopor €130,000, and Dupont €105,944 represent 89% of total costs
  • Closure Performance: 75% of CAPs closed within 3 months, with average closure time of 82 days
  • Root Cause Analysis: Method-related issues 46% dominate the 7M cause categories
  • Yearly Trend: 2024 showed highest activity with 17 CAPs and €388,451 in costs

Overview and Data Summary

The CAP data spans from February 2023 to September 2025, covering 37 unique corrective action plans across multiple suppliers, customers, and quality issue types. All CAPs fall under the NVSA Non-Conformity Verification category, indicating systematic quality management processes.

Strategic Recommendations

Based on the comprehensive analysis, the following strategic recommendations are proposed:

1. Supplier Quality Management

Critical Priority: Address the 88.6% cost concentration in top 3 suppliers

Actions:

  • Develop supplier quality agreements with Mitsubishi, Inopor, and Dupont
  • Implement incoming quality inspection for high-value equipment
  • Establish alternative sourcing strategies for critical components
  • Create joint quality improvement programs with key suppliers

Expected Impact: 50-70% reduction in high-cost CAPs within 12-18 months

2. Method and Process Improvement

Priority: Address 46% of CAPs caused by method-related issues

Actions:

  • Review and update Standard Operating Procedures SOPs
  • Enhance training programs for installation and commissioning teams
  • Implement process validation and verification protocols
  • Create checklists and quality control points for critical steps

Expected Impact: 30-40% reduction in method-related CAPs within 6-12 months

3. CAP Closure Time Optimization

Priority: Reduce closure time variance 45.8-day standard deviation

Actions:

  • Implement CAP complexity scoring for more accurate closure date estimation
  • Establish CAP management team with dedicated resources
  • Create CAP tracking dashboard with automated alerts
  • Standardize CAP documentation and approval processes

Expected Impact: Improve closure time consistency, reduce average to 60-70 days

4. Predictive CAP Prevention

Priority: Reduce overall CAP volume by addressing root causes

Actions:

  • Develop CAP trend analysis and early warning system
  • Implement preventive maintenance programs for high-risk equipment
  • Create customer-specific risk profiles and mitigation plans
  • Establish lessons learned database and knowledge sharing platform

Expected Impact: 20-30% reduction in CAP volume over 2 years

5. Customer Quality Partnership

Priority: Address customer scene issues 18.9% of CAPs

Actions:

  • Develop customer-specific installation and operation guidelines
  • Implement joint customer training programs
  • Create customer feedback integration system
  • Establish proactive customer quality audits

Expected Impact: 25-35% reduction in customer-related CAPs

Conclusion

The CAP analysis for 2023-2025 reveals a well-structured quality management system with strong closure performance 75.7% under 3 months but significant opportunities for improvement in cost control and prevention.

Critical Success Factors:

1. Cost Concentration: Address top 3 suppliers 88.6% of costs for immediate financial impact

2. Method Issues: Tackle 46% of CAPs through process improvement

3. Performance Variance: Standardize CAP management for consistent results

4. Predictive Prevention: Move from reactive to proactive quality management

Financial Impact Potential:

  • Short-term 12 months: €200,000-300,000 cost reduction through supplier quality improvements
  • Medium-term 24 months: €400,000-500,000 cost reduction through method improvements and prevention
  • Long-term 36+ months: €600,000+ total cost reduction with comprehensive quality management system

The data suggests that while the current CAP management system is effective in closing issues, there is significant opportunity to prevent CAPs before they occur, reduce their cost impact, and improve overall quality performance through strategic interventions focused on supplier quality, method improvement, and predictive quality management.
