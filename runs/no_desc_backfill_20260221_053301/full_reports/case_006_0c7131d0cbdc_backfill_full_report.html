Portland CDBG Funding Analysis 2016-2024

Executive Summary

This report provides a comprehensive analysis of Community Development Block Grant CDBG allocations in Portland, Maine from fiscal year 2016/17 through 2023/24. The analysis covers 253 funding applications across 114 organizations, totaling 21.6 million in requested funding with 4.7 million actually awarded.

Key Findings:

  • Total Funding Awarded: 4,705,753 across 8 fiscal years
  • Overall Award Rate: 21.8% of requested funds
  • Peak Funding Period: 2018/19 with 64.7% award rate 1.6M awarded
  • Funding Gap: 2019/20 to 2023/24 - 12.9M requested but 0 awarded
  • Top Recipient: COP Public Works 804,367 over 8 projects

Timeline Analysis

Active Funding Period 2016/17 - 2018/19

The first three years show robust funding activity:

  • 2016/17: 3.1M requested, 1.5M awarded 48.9% award rate
  • 2017/18: 4.1M requested, 1.6M awarded 38.4% award rate
  • 2018/19: 2.5M requested, 1.6M awarded 64.7% award rate

This period represents 100% of all awarded funding 4.7M total, with a cumulative award rate of 47.3%.

Funding Gap Period 2019/20 - 2023/24

Starting in 2019/20, a complete halt in funding allocations occurred despite continued application submissions:

  • 2019/20: 2.1M requested, 0 awarded 0.0% award rate
  • 2020/21: 3.4M requested, 0 awarded 0.0% award rate
  • 2021/22: 2.0M requested, 0 awarded 0.0% award rate
  • 2022/23: 2.2M requested, 0 awarded 0.0% award rate
  • 2023/24: 2.2M requested, 0 awarded 0.0% award rate

During this five-year period, organizations submitted 151 applications requesting 12.9M, but received no funding. This represents 60% of all applications in the dataset.

Key Insights and Recommendations

1. Funding Gap Analysis

The complete cessation of funding from 2019/20 through 2023/24 represents a critical issue requiring investigation. Potential causes may include:

  • Federal funding reductions or reallocation
  • Changes in program administration or priorities
  • COVID-19 pandemic impacts on federal budgets
  • Local policy changes or budget constraints
  • Data collection or reporting issues

Recommendation: Conduct a thorough review of CDBG program administration during this period to understand the root cause and develop strategies to restore funding.

2. Application Success Patterns

The varying award rates across application types and priorities reveal important patterns:

  • SET applicationshave the highest success rate 56.3%, suggesting strong alignment with program objectives
  • Social Services SShas the highest volume but lowest award rate 17.3%, indicating either high demand or lower priority
  • Priority NIreceives the most funding, reflecting its status as a core objective

Recommendation: Provide clearer guidance to applicants about funding priorities and success factors to improve application quality and alignment.

3. Organizational Capacity Building

The concentration of funding among top recipients suggests:

  • Established partners have stronger application capabilities
  • Capacity building could help new organizations compete more effectively
  • Technical assistance programs may improve success rates

Recommendation: Develop capacity-building programs for organizations, particularly those serving high-need populations, to improve application quality and program implementation.

4. Infrastructure vs. Services Balance

The funding split between infrastructure SET, CON and services SS, ED shows:

  • Infrastructure projects: 60.7% of awarded funds 2.9M
  • Service projects: 39.3% of awarded funds 1.8M

Recommendation: Evaluate the community needs balance between physical infrastructure and social services to ensure optimal allocation of limited resources.

5. Future Planning Considerations

Given the five-year funding gap:

  • Organizations should diversify funding sources
  • Long-term planning should account for funding uncertainty
  • Program sustainability strategies are essential

Recommendation: Develop comprehensive sustainability plans for CDBG-funded programs that include diverse funding sources and contingency planning.

Conclusion

The Portland CDBG program has provided substantial funding to community development initiatives over the eight-year period analyzed. However, the significant funding gap from 2019-2024 represents a critical challenge that requires immediate attention.

Key takeaways:

1. 4.7M awardedto 253 applications from 2016-2019

2. 12.9M in unmet needsfrom 2019-2024 applications

3. 21.8% overall award ratewith significant variation by category

4. Top 15 organizationsreceived 80% of funding

5. Infrastructure projectsreceived 60.7% of awarded funds

The program demonstrates success in supporting community development when funding is available, but recent years show a concerning trend of unmet community needs. Addressing the funding gap and improving program sustainability should be top priorities for program administrators and community stakeholders.
