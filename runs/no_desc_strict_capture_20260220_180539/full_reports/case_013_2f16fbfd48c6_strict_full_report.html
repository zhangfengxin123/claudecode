1.1 The Kakeya Conjecture Breakthrough

The Kakeya conjecture, which remained open for nearly a century, was finally solved for the 3D case by Wang-Zahl in 2025. This represents:

  • A monumental achievement for Chinese mathematics
  • The culmination of methodological innovations spanning geometric, arithmetic, and algebraic approaches
  • A key example of the "divide and conquer" philosophy combined with scale induction and projection theory

Historical progression:

  • Wolff 1990s: Proved dim ≥ 5/2 for 3D Kakeya sets using hairbrush method
  • Katz-Laba-Tao 2000: Improved to dim ≥ 5/2 + ε₀ overcoming theoretical barriers
  • Wang-Zahl 2022: Solved the "sticky" case
  • Wang-Zahl 2025: Complete resolution of 3D case

Research Methodology Evolution:

The field has witnessed three major methodological paradigms:

1. Geometric Methods 1970s-1980s: Pioneered by Córdoba, Fefferman, and Zygmund

Core principle: Two nonparallel lines intersect at most once

Tools: Incidence geometry, additive combinatorics

Success: 2D Restriction conjecture

2. Arithmetic Methods 1990s-2000s: Revolutionized by Bourgain

Core concept: Sumsets, difference sets, arithmetic progressions

Cross-disciplinary bridge: Number theory meets harmonic analysis

Impact: Methodological breakthrough enabling major advances

3. Algebraic Geometry Methods 2000s-present: Led by Dvir, Guth, and Wang-Zahl

Core tool: Algebraic polynomials and their properties

Power: Broad applicability beyond harmonic analysis

Landmark result: 3D Kakeya conjecture resolution

8. Research Methods: Strengths and Limitations

8.1 Geometric Methods

Strengths:

  • Intuitive visualization
  • Effective for low-dimensional problems
  • Clear geometric interpretation

Limitations:

  • Difficult to extend to high dimensions
  • Limited power for complex configurations
  • Insufficient for modern problems

8.2 Arithmetic Methods

Strengths:

  • Cross-disciplinary innovation
  • Powerful for additive problems
  • Connects harmonic analysis to number theory

Limitations:

  • Requires deep number theory background
  • Complex technical machinery
  • Limited applicability in some geometric contexts

8.3 Algebraic Geometry Methods

Strengths:

  • Broad applicability beyond harmonic analysis
  • Handles complex geometric structures
  • Proven power in landmark results 3D Kakeya

Limitations:

  • Requires algebraic geometry expertise
  • Results may not always be optimal
  • Computationally intensive

8.4 Decoupling Theory

Strengths:

  • Unifying framework
  • Wide applications number theory, geometric measure theory, PDEs
  • Powerful for high-codimension problems

Limitations:

  • Technically complex
  • Requires sophisticated machinery
  • Ongoing refinement needed
