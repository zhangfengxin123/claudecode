Executive Summary

This comprehensive analysis examines 6,607 student records to identify key factors driving exam performance. Through exploratory data analysis, correlation analysis, and multiple linear regression modeling, we discovered that attendance rate 58.8% impactand hours studied 44.9% impactare the strongest predictors of exam scores.

The regression model achieves an R² of 0.598 with a root mean square error of 2.47 points, indicating strong predictive power. Students with access to educational resources score 1.89 points higher on average than those without, while parental involvement contributes an additional 1.73 points.

Key Findings

  • Most Critical Factor: Attendance correlation: 0.581, standardized coefficient: 0.588
  • Second Most Important: Hours Studied correlation: 0.445, standardized coefficient: 0.449
  • Top Categorical Impact: Access to Resources +1.89 points, Parental Involvement +1.73 points
  • Negative Impact: Learning Disabilities -1.08 points
  • Model Performance: R² = 0.598, RMSE = 2.47, MAE = 1.31

Limitations and Future Work

  • Categorical effects are analyzed separately but not integrated into the predictive model
  • The dataset lacks temporal data to track individual progress over time
