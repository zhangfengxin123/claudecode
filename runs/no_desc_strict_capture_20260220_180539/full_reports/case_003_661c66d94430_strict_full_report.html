Executive Summary

This analysis examines VideoDR- the first video deep research benchmark designed to evaluate agentic video reasoning capabilities in open-web environments. The benchmark addresses a critical gap in multimodal AI evaluation by requiring models to:

1. Extract cross-frame visual anchors from videos

2. Perform iterative web retrieval on open web

3. Conduct multi-hop reasoning over joint video-web evidence

Key Finding: The Agentic paradigm is not consistently superior to Workflow - its advantages depend heavily on a model's ability to maintain video anchors through long retrieval chains. The core bottlenecks identified are goal driftand long-horizon consistency.

Research Context and Motivation

The Challenge

Existing video reasoning evaluations operate in closed-evidence settings, where models only need to answer questions within a given video. However, real-world video question answering scenarios present a different challenge:

  • Videos provide localized visual cuesonly
  • Verifiable answers are distributed across the open web
  • Models must perform cross-frame clue extraction
  • Iterative retrieval and multi-hop reasoningare required for verification

VideoDR bridges this gap by extending deep research to an open-domain factoid question answering setting conditioned on video.

Benchmark Construction Methodology

The VideoDR benchmark follows a rigorous five-stage pipeline:

Data Quality Controls

1. Video & Web Dependency Testing

  • Tests ensure samples cannot be answered by video alone or webpages alone
  • Discards samples that fail dependency requirements

2. Human Testing

  • Five independent participants solve tasks
  • Blind evaluation to validate solvability
  • Human performance baseline: 50.4% success rate ±34.6%

3. Difficulty Stratification

  • Low: 0-1 correct answers 65% human success
  • Mid: 2-3 correct answers 50% human success
  • High: 4-5 correct answers 36% human success

Key Challenges Identified

1. Goal Drift

Models lose track of original video anchors during long retrieval chains. This manifests as:

  • Search queries divergingfrom initial visual cues
  • Evidence collection strayingfrom task requirements
  • Final answers disconnectedfrom video context

2. Long-Horizon Consistency

Maintaining coherence across multi-step reasoning chains is challenging:

  • Accumulating errorsacross retrieval-reasoning cycles
  • Inconsistent evidenceinterpretation across steps
  • Context window limitationsfor complex tasks

3. Video-Web Integration

Effectively combining video and web evidence requires:

  • Balanced weightingof visual and textual evidence
  • Cross-modal reasoningcapabilities
  • Confidence calibrationacross evidence sources

Implications for Future Research

For Model Development

1. Anchor Maintenance Mechanisms: Develop better methods to maintain visual anchors throughout retrieval chains

2. Goal Tracking Systems: Implement explicit goal-state representations to prevent drift

3. Hierarchical Reasoning: Design architectures that support long-horizon consistency

4. Cross-Modal Integration: Improve video-web evidence fusion capabilities

For Benchmark Design

1. Longer Retrieval Chains: Test models on even longer reasoning chains

2. Domain Generalization: Expand to more diverse semantic domains

3. Adversarial Testing: Include adversarial samples that specifically trigger goal drift

4. Granular Evaluation: Evaluate intermediate steps, not just final accuracy

Limitations and Future Work

Current Limitations

1. Benchmark Scale: Limited by manual annotation requirements

2. Static Evaluation: Does not test real-time video processing

3. Web Dependency: Performance may vary as web content changes

4. English-only: Currently focuses on English-language content

Future Directions

1. Automated Annotation: Develop methods to scale up benchmark construction

2. Temporal Reasoning: Better evaluate temporal understanding in videos

3. Multilingual Support: Expand to multiple languages

4. Interactive Evaluation: Test models in real-time video-web environments

Conclusion

VideoDR represents a significant advancement in evaluating agentic video reasoning capabilities. The benchmark reveals that:

1. Video deep research is fundamentally challenging- even the best models GPT-5.2 achieve only 50%

2. Agentic paradigm has advantages- but these depend on model's ability to maintain video anchors through long retrieval chains

3. Core bottlenecks are goal drift and long-horizon consistency- not just individual component performance

4. Significant improvement needed- closed-source outperform open-source by 15 percentage points

The benchmark provides a systematic framework for studying video agents in open-web settings and identifies clear directions for next-generation video deep research agents.
